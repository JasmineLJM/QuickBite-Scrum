# Agile Reflection 

Our QuickBite Restaurant Pickup Platform demonstrated agility through incremental delivery, responding to change, stakeholder collabration, working software, and continuous improvement. The team used feedback and testing results to improve the product throughout development. 

## 1. Incremental Delivery 
The team developed QuickBite incrementally across three Sprints. Sprint 1 focused on the core customer ordering workflow, including restaurant selection, menu browsing, adding menu items, pickup time selection, and order submission. Sprint 2 added restaurant order management, customer order tracking, and input validation. Sprint 3 introduced restaurant pause controls. 

Each Sprint contributed new functionality to the application, allowing the team to build on previous work instead of completing every feature at once. 

## 2. Responding to Change 
The team demonstrated flexibility when restaurant stakeholders requested a way to manage incoming orders based on restaurant workload. After discussing the requirement, the team agreed to implement manual restaurant pause controls instead of workload-based pickup slot restrictions. 

The Product Backlog was updated to reflect this change, and the team implemented independent pause controls for each restaurant. The final feature supported selectable pause durations, cumulative extensions through repeated clicks, and visible pause status or countdowns. 

This showed the team's ability to adapt to the changes based on stakeholder needs. 

## 3. Stakeholder Collaboration 
The team demonstrated stakeholder collaboration through simulated stakeholder discussions and the Sprint Review.

During Part F, the team discussed the restaurant workload requirement and agreed to implement manual pause controls. 

During Part G, team members simulated customer and restaurant stakeholders and provided feedback on the application. The team reviewed the feedback, recorded decisions, and updated the Product Backlog. 

This showed how stakeholder feedback influenced the team's decisions and helped refine the product throughout development. 

## 4. Working Software 
The team prioritized delivering a functional application. QuickBite was implemented using HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. 

The working application allowed customers to browse restaurants, select menu items, choose pickup times, submit orders, and view order progress. Restaurant employees could view incoming orders, update order statuses, and temporarily pause new orders. 

The team showed these features during the Sprint Review, providing concrete evidence of a working increment. 

## 5. Continuous Improvement 
The Sprint Retrospective gave the team an opportunity to reflect on the development process and identify specific improvements. 

The team agreed to strengthen cross-broswer compatibility testing by checking customer and restaurant workflows in three different browsers. The team also planned to improve task coordination by assigning responsibilities clearly, identifying dependencies, and reviewing GitHub Project progress during Daily Scrums. 

These planned improvements showed how the team used its experience to identify practical changes for future development and testing. 

## Conclusion 
Overall, the QuickBite project showed agility through delivering working software incrementally, adapting to stakeholder feedback, and continuously improving both the product and the team's development practices. 
Scrum activities provided opportunities for collaboration and reflection, but the strongest evidence of agility was how our team used feedback, testing, and changing requirements to make decisions.
